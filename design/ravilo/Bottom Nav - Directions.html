Directions · phone only · 2026-09-18

Ravilo's pages move to the bottom

R267 gave the phone a two-row top bar — identity and actions above, pages below — and recorded as its open question 1 whether the pages belong at the bottom instead. Owner direction, 2026-09-18: put them at the bottom. This canvas draws four ways to do that, on one axis: how the selected page is marked, because that is the only thing the four differ on that a viewer would ever notice. Everything else — what the top bar keeps, what the bar does in the player, where the cast mini bar goes — is decided once, below, and is the same in all four.

Nothing here is built into Ravilo Mobile.html yet. Pick a direction and the build follows; R267 then gains the bottom bar and its FR-R267-5 (the page row) is superseded.

Decided before any direction

These are consequences of moving the pages, not choices between the four. They are the same in every frame.

DecidedWhy
The top bar keeps its one rowBrand · cast (when the server says Chromecast is set up, R245 FR-R245-1) · search · avatar — R267's row one, unchanged, still no clock (the platform draws one in the status bar directly above). Moving the pages down empties row two; it does not change row one.
Four items, never fiveHome · Movies · Series · Discover — raviloNavItems(). My List stays in the avatar menu (R170) and Search stays an icon (R52). Four is what makes a bottom bar readable; a fifth would force icon-only or a scroller, and a bottom bar that scrolls is a broken bottom bar.
Discover's five chips stay at the top of DiscoverThey are that page's own content (R243/R262/R268: Networks · Studios · Genres · Coming Soon · Request, scrollable). They are not app navigation and must not be confused with it — which is exactly why the two rows of chips stacked on top of each other read badly today.
Absent in the player, and on any pushed screenDetail pages, the player, the cast remote, the profile picker and a seeded grid have their own Back and no nav — the bar belongs to the four pages only. It never overlays video.
The cast mini bar sits directly above itR245 FR-R245-6: 64 dp, never dismissible while a cast runs. It docks on top of the nav bar, the pair moves as one, and the mini bar keeps the tap target it has today. This is the collision R267 OQ1 worried about, and it resolves by stacking — see §3.
Safe areas, targets, type floorThe bar sits above the home indicator / gesture pill, every item is a ≥ 46 dp target, no label under 13 sp… except where a direction says otherwise — see the warning below.
Always visible on its four pagesNo hide-on-scroll. It is the one affordance that says where you are; a bar that disappears while you read a row is a bar you have to go looking for.
One real tension, and it is a rule this project already set. R234/R244 put a 13 px floor on any label a viewer reads. Platform bottom bars habitually use 11–12 px, and the four directions below are drawn at 11.5 px (A, B) and 11 px (C) to look like what people expect — so either the floor bends for a permanent, four-word, icon-paired label, or the labels go up to 13 px and the bar gets ~4 px taller. Direction D avoids the question by only ever showing one label, at 13 px. Flagged as decision 2.
Baseline · today

Two rows at the top

What R267 built and what this replaces: row one is identity and actions, row two is the pages. It works, it is honest, and it costs two of the four thumb-reachable rows of the screen to say where you are — while the pages themselves sit at the far end of the reach.

What moving them down buys

Reach. The four pages become the four easiest taps on the phone. Today they sit ~800 px from the thumb on a 915 px screen.
One row back. The top bar drops to a single 56 px row, so the hero starts ~48 px higher and one more poster row lands above the fold.
No more two-strips-stacked confusion. Discover's own five chips are the only chip row on screen once the app's pages are glyphs at the bottom — the ambiguity R262 §3 and R268 both dance around disappears.
It is what the platform does. A bottom bar is the shape every phone viewer already knows, and the one Android's own predictive-back and gesture model is built around.

And what it costs

A row of screen, permanently. ~74 px of the 915 is gone on every page — that is the trade, and it is why the bar must not also carry a clock, a search field or a fifth item.
Two navigation idioms in one app. The TV keeps a top row; the phone gets a bottom bar. Defensible (they are different devices, and R256/R267 already split on form factor) but it is a second layout to maintain in AppBar.kt's file.
The mini bar has to move. It docks above the nav instead of over the content — drawn in §3, and the one thing to check on a real phone before the phase is closed.
§2 · the axis

How the selected page is marked

All four are the same bar: four items, icon over label, 56–60 px tall, above the gesture pill, always visible. They differ only in how selected reads — which on a phone is the whole job, because there is no focus ring and no hover to help.
A · Standard

Ink and weight

Selected = full-ink icon and label, heavier stroke; the rest muted. A hairline above, the page's own background below. The most conventional thing possible, and the cheapest to build.

Why B. On a phone the selected state has no help from focus or hover, so it has to be carried by a shape, not by two shades of grey — that rules out A. It names every page, which D does not, and Discover is exactly the label a viewer cannot infer from a glyph. It stays opaque, so nothing slides under it and the mini bar docks against a straight edge — which C cannot promise. And it is the one direction where Ravilo's gradient does real work instead of decorating: the brand marks where you are.
Why not C, concretely. A translucent capsule over a scrolling poster wall is the one place in this app where ink sits on a picture — the exact trap the casting round rejected direction 1 for (“ink on a solid surface always clears 4.5:1; a scrimmed photo does not”). It is the prettiest frame on this canvas and the least defensible one.

§4 · round 2 · 2026-09-18

Search wants the middle, and Movies/Series don't want to be split by it

Owner: Search in the middle, Home on the left — and Movies and Series sitting either side of Search reads badly, which it does: they are a pair and the bar breaks them up. A centre slot needs an odd number of items, so the question is really what the other slots are. Four compositions below; the bars are drawn at real phone width. Movies and Series are the same screen in the app already — Dest.Browse(kind) with a type parameter — so merging them is a rename plus one toggle, not a new screen.

The four compositions

OptionThe barWhat it costs, and what it buys
1 · ThreeHome · Search · DiscoverSearch is literally the centre, Home is left, nothing is split. Movies and Series become two more Discover segments (so Discover reads Movies · Series · Networks · Studios · Genres · Coming Soon · Request). Cheapest bar, most phone-like. Cost: "show me all films" goes from one tap to two, and Discover starts to look like a drawer with seven chips in it.
2 · Library (rec.)Home · Library · Search · Discover · My ListThe pair merges into one Library page whose first row is a Movies / Series / All toggle — which is what the code already does. Five slots, Search dead centre, Home left, nothing split, and the fifth slot goes to My List, which on a phone is a real destination and is currently two taps deep in the avatar menu (R170 put it there for a TV, where the avatar is two D-pad presses away). Cost: one new label, Library, in three languages.
3 · FourHome · Library · Search · DiscoverSame merge, no fifth item. Honest and tidy — but Search is the third of four, i.e. just right of centre, not in it. If the middle matters, four items cannot give it to you.
4 · Keep bothHome · Movies · Search · Series · DiscoverWhat is built now. Everything stays one tap; the pair is split by a control that has nothing to do with either. Included so the comparison is fair — it is the only option where "all series" is one tap and no new string is written.
Recommendation: option 2. It is the only one that satisfies both of the owner's constraints (Home left, Search centre) and keeps every destination one tap away — and the merge it depends on is not a redesign: Dest.Browse(kind) is already one screen with a parameter, so Library is that screen with its parameter exposed as a toggle instead of as two nav items. The fifth slot then pays for itself: My List is the household's own shelf, it is the thing a viewer opens on a phone while the TV is busy, and nothing else in the app competes for that slot (Live TV is woven into Home by config, never a tab — R177).
If a new string is the objection, option 1 is the fallback, not option 4: it writes no new label either (Movies and Series already exist as words, they just move into Discover's strip) and it keeps the middle slot. The thing to weigh is whether Discover can carry seven chips without becoming a drawer — R268 already made that strip scrollable, so it fits; whether it reads is the judgement call.
