Ravilo · design directions · round 2

Same information. No popover.

The plate solved the problem by putting a panel on top of the page, and a floating panel is the one thing a 10-foot UI has no good precedent for. So here are six directions that carry the same fields and never overlay anything.

What separates them is not styling, it's where the information lives. Once you name that, the choice mostly makes itself.

In a fixed region

A zone of the page that always describes the focused title. Rows never move. → G, L

In the page itself

The whole screen becomes the title; rows retreat to a strip. → H

In a second column

Detail and list side by side, permanently. → I

In the row

The shelf you're standing in grows to hold the text. → J, K

The plate (Direction A) is now off by default in the mockups — the switch in Preferences → Focus detail turns it back on if you want to compare against it. All six directions read the same resolved fields (fieldsFor()), so choosing one is a rendering change, not a data one.

Read them in this order: G is the safe one and my recommendation. K is the quietest thing that could work. H is the one with real presence. I changes what Home is. J feels best under the thumb and carries the one hardware risk. L is the minimum viable answer, drawn so you can see how little is enough.

Direction G · recommended

The band — a fixed region that always describes the focused title

G · band above, rows below · Mýrin focused in row 1

G · before you enter a row — the household's pick

The hero stops being a carousel and becomes a permanent 380px information band. Whatever has focus, the band describes it: title at 64px, meta, synopsis, genres, flags, artwork on the right. Rows sit below and scroll under nothing.

This is the one that fixes the actual complaint. Nothing floats, nothing covers a tile, nothing appears or disappears — the text simply changes in place as you move, the way a station's now-playing panel does. The page has exactly one place where information lives, and you learn it in two seconds.

It also retires an oddity you already have: a hero advertising a title you didn't ask about on a 6.5s timer. Before you enter a row, the band shows the household's pick — after that, it's yours.

Cost: a layout change, not a new surface. Rows start ~140px lower, so Home shows 2 full rows instead of 2½. Zero risk to focus traversal.

Direction K · the quiet one

The caption grows

K · only the focused tile's caption expands

No new region at all. The tile's own caption — today one line of year · genre — expands for the focused tile only: title, meta, three lines of synopsis, the flag strip. The row band grows ~150px; the tiles themselves never move.

Cheapest possible answer, and the most native: the information is attached to the thing, in the place captions already live, so there's nothing new to learn and nothing to dismiss. On a phone or the web it needs no separate treatment.

The limit is honest: a caption is 340px wide, so the synopsis clamps to three short lines and the genres have to sit on one row. If the answer to "what is this?" needs more words than that, K can't give them and G can.

Watch: the row's height changes as focus enters and leaves it, which nudges the rows below. Pinning the row height to the tallest caption avoids that at the cost of permanent empty space.

Direction H · presence

Cinema — the page becomes the title

H · Big Buck Bunny focused · one row, dimmed, at the foot

The focused title's artwork goes full-bleed. Its name is set at 84px where a poster would be, the synopsis under it, and the row you're browsing retreats to a single dimmed strip at the bottom — neighbours at 50% opacity, only the focused tile at full.

This is the most confident of the six and by far the best-looking on a big screen in a dark room. It also makes browsing feel like choosing rather than scrolling a catalogue, because you see one title at a time at full size.

Two real costs. You lose sight of the rest of the library — only one row is visible, so the "what else is there" browse loop gets slower. And it depends entirely on artwork: with only a gradient behind it, this direction is much weaker than G, and most of this project's fictional titles have no art.

Needs: a backdrop per focused title — the one direction that can't degrade gracefully without one.

Direction I · a different Home

Split — detail column beside the list

I · fixed detail pane left, rows right

The page divides permanently: a fixed 772px detail pane on the left, rows on the right in a narrower track. Left never scrolls, right never covers. The most information of any direction — art, synopsis, and a facts grid all visible at once, without a single overlay.

It's the classic 10-foot inspector pattern and it's genuinely good for a household that browses deliberately. It also gives the detail page's own content a permanent home, which makes Select feel like less of a leap.

But it changes what Home is. Only ~3 tiles per row are visible, poster art gets small, and every existing row layout, the channel rail and the 6-up browse grid would need re-thinking. This is a redesign of the browse experience, not an addition to it — worth doing only if that's what you want.

Cost: the largest by far. Touches every row surface, the rail, browse, search. Not a phase — a project.

Direction J

The row opens

J · the active row grows; the rows around it don't

The row you're standing in becomes the detail surface. The focused poster grows to 300×450 in place, the text sits beside it inside the row's own band, and the rest of the row continues past it at normal size. Other rows are untouched.

The appeal: information appears exactly where you're looking, with no new region and no overlay, and the row still reads as a shelf — you can see what's to the right of the thing you're considering, which H and I both lose.

The catch is the one C had. The row's height and the tiles' positions change on every focus move, which is the reflow that invariant 11 exists to protect — and unlike C, this one moves the row's height too, so the rows below shift. It needs a measurement on the stue BRAVIA before it's promised.

Mitigation if you like it: pin the open row's band height permanently so only widths animate, never the page's vertical rhythm.

Direction L · the minimum

One line at the foot of the screen

L · status line, rows otherwise untouched

A permanent 88px strip at the bottom: title, year, seasons, runtime, rating, IMDb, flags, and where you left off. It never moves, never covers a tile, and is readable at a glance without pulling your eye off the row.

Drawn because it's the honest floor of this whole exercise. If the household's real question is "how long is it and is there Danish audio", this answers it completely, costs almost nothing, and can't regress anything.

What it can't do is describe a title — there's no room for a synopsis, which is the field that actually decides "what is this?". Good as a companion to K; not a substitute for G.

Cheapest to ship, cheapest to reverse. One row of text, no layout change beyond 88px of bottom padding.

What I'd pick, and why

G, with K's caption as a later refinement. G is the only direction that gives the synopsis room, keeps every row where it is, needs no artwork to work, and carries no hardware risk — and it removes the hero carousel, which is currently the least useful 550px on the screen. If G's band feels too heavy once it's live, K is the fallback that needs no new region at all. H is the one I'd build if you told me artwork was coming; I only if you want to redesign browsing outright.
