Round 1 · directions only · 2026-09-16

Casting from the phone to a Chromecast

Against design brief §B / §C / §F. The whole flow as a storyboard, two directions for the full-screen remote, the mini bar, the ten screens the TV shows while it is being cast to, and the admin card that turns the feature on.

One architectural fact decides almost every frame here: the receiver is its own Ravilo device, not a screen the phone drives. It holds its own device token, runs its own heartbeats, advances to the next episode and honours Skip Intro by itself. So the phone is a remote in the literal sense — it can die, and the TV keeps playing.

What the architecture already decided

Five things are settled by the investigation and the owner's answers. They are not design choices, and three of them are the reason this is not just "a remote screen".

SettledConsequence for these frames
The TV keeps playing if the phone diesThe remote is never the source of truth. On app start it rebuilds from the receiver — item, position, tracks, next-up — never from anything the phone remembered. That is why the re-connect moment is drawn as a first-class screen and not an edge case.
Re-connect has two outcomes, and one of them is silenceCast still running → the mini bar appears with the live position. Receiver finished or gone → nothing is shown and no error is raised; the viewer just casts again with one tap. An "your cast ended" toast would be noise about something they already know.
The cast button is server-pushedIt exists only when the server says Chromecast is set up. Absent, never greyed — a disabled control is a promise you are not keeping. Off in Settings ⇒ the phone is told there is nothing to cast to and never guesses.
The receiver appears in Users & devicesIt enrols with a hand-off code and gets its own row, so phase 110 gives it a named Jellyfin session — which is why the owner's "control the pause from the Jellyfin dashboard" already works once this exists. Nothing on the phone has to implement it.
Every cast is a Jellyfin encodeA Cast receiver never direct-plays MKV. So R222's slow-to-start note, R218's waiting states and R182's busy state all apply to casting, and the remote has to hold them — drawn as three of its seven states. The viewer is still never told why.
The one thing the admin pays for is named. Google is the only product named anywhere in this canvas, and only on the admin card, because the admin registers a Cast application with Google for US$5. Nothing in the viewer's half names a product, a protocol or a status code.

Read it in this order

§B1 is the flow, drawn as nine phone frames left to right — there is only one design, so there is nothing to choose there; it either reads or it does not. The choice in this canvas is the remote (§B2): two directions, on the axis of whether the artwork is the screen or a card on it.

Recommendation: Remote 2 · Now playing. The art is a 16:9 card on a solid ground instead of a dimmed full-bleed backdrop. Three reasons, in order of weight: (1) ink on a solid surface always clears 4.5:1 — a scrimmed backdrop does not, and the household's library mostly has no backdrop at all, so direction 1's best case is its rarest case; (2) the device you are controlling belongs at the top, as a chip that is also how you switch or stop — direction 1 buries Stop casting in the bottom row with everything else; (3) it degrades to a wordmark tile with no redraw, the same rule R243's taxonomy walls already follow.

Then §C — the receiver's ten screens, at 1920 × 1080 scaled to a third. They are deliberately the dullest thing in this document: nothing on them is interactive, so every one is a single sentence or a single overlay.

Then §F — the admin card in three states, in app/settings.html's own idiom.

Not drawn, on purpose

No AirPlay affordance (dropped, 2026-09-16). No device picker of our own — that dialog is the platform's on both phones and redesigning it would be a lie about what ships. No volume slider on the remote (hardware keys plus a transient pill). No Cast Connect (later phase). No "Play on Stue TV" — it is phase 111's own thing and belongs with §D.

Brief §B1 · storyboard

From "I want this on the TV" to "the TV is playing it"

Nine frames, Android portrait. The owner's requirement is that starting a cast be super simple and fast: the cast icon is on every app bar, one tap opens the system picker, and pressing Play while connected casts instead of playing locally. No interstitial, no setup step, no "connecting…" screen that owns the whole view.
1

The cast icon, on every app bar

Detail screen, not casting yet. The icon sits between Search and the avatar. Three states: idle outline · connecting (the bars fill in sequence) · connected (filled, accent-tinted). It is absent entirely when Chromecast is off server-side.
2

The system picker — not ours

One tap. This dialog belongs to the platform on both Android and iOS; we draw the frame around it so the moment is understood and then leave it alone. Drawn as Android's; iOS's is a sheet with the same content.
3

Connecting

A thin bar under the app bar, nothing more — the app stays usable while it connects. "Connecting to Living room TV…" becomes "Casting to Living room TV" and the bar retires itself after ~2 s.
4

Play casts, and opens the remote

While connected, the detail screen's primary action says Play on Living room TV. Pressing it hands the item and the resume position over and pushes the remote. Pressing the cast icon inside a local player hands the current position over instead.
5

Re-connect · the moment

App opened cold, a cast already running. The icon animates and one bar reads "Reconnecting to Living room TV…". This is the requirement of §B1.5, not a nicety: the phone may have been dead, and the TV never stopped.
6

…outcome A: it is still playing

The mini bar appears with the live position read from the receiver, and one tap opens the remote. This frame is also §B3: the mini bar is what casting looks like from every other screen in the app.
7

…outcome B: it has finished or gone

Nothing. No bar, no toast, no error — just the app, with the cast icon back to idle. The viewer already knows the film ended; telling them is a notification about the past.
8

Stop casting

From the remote's footer or from the cast icon. The mini bar goes, the icon returns to idle, and the TV returns to the receiver's idle screen. No confirmation dialog — it is one tap to start again.
9

Lock screen · §B4

The Cast SDK's own notification, so the layout is the platform's. Ours to decide: the artwork crop and the copy. Landscape still, not poster — a poster crops to nothing at 64 px — and the device name on the second line where the artist would be.

Both share these rules

  • Volume is the hardware keys plus a transient pill. No on-screen slider — on a cast, volume belongs to the TV and its own remote, and a slider invites a fight between them.
  • Dragging the seek bar is allowed, and the position is optimistic: the thumb moves at once, the receiver confirms, and a disagreement resolves to the receiver's number.
  • Subtitles & audio opens the same sheet as the local player — the picker from R180/R195, applied on the TV. One component, two destinations.
  • Back / edge swipe leaves the remote, never the cast. Stopping is only ever explicit.
State 2

Paused

Play glyph, and the art card dims slightly so the state is legible without reading a button. Nothing else moves.
State 3

Buffering on the TV

R218 moment C, borrowed: the spinner stands in for the play button. The phone is fine; the TV is waiting. No explanation, because the viewer cannot act on one.
State 4

Next-up, mirrored

The receiver owns the countdown — it advances by itself. The remote mirrors it and can cancel or skip ahead. This is the clearest demonstration that the TV is not being driven by the phone.
State 5

Receiver unreachable

Lost contact with the TV. One sentence, one action. The transport greys because pressing it would be a lie. The cast may well still be playing — we simply cannot see it, and the copy says exactly that much and no more.
State 8 · the one you asked for

Subtitles & audio, from the remote

The same sheet as the local player — R180/R195's two-level flag-forward picker, one component, two destinations — except the choice is applied on the TV. One line says so, and Subtitle size is the same S · M · L, which is why that row lives in the picker rather than on the rail. This is the whole answer to "change subtitles while casting": one tap from the remote, no trip back into the app.
State 6

Server busy · phase 182's 503

The one state that has a number in it: the elapsed wait, because a wait you can see the length of is a different experience from one you cannot. Still no cause, still no product named.
State 7

Ended

The title finished and there is nothing next. The remote says so and offers the two things that are actually available. The TV, meanwhile, has gone back to its idle screen.

Receiver rules worth writing down

  • It never talks about settings. Same rule the TV app follows (R216/R234). Nothing on this screen can be changed from this screen, so nothing on it should describe a control.
  • Idle is the lit mark and one sentence. Not a carousel, not a screensaver, not artwork from the library — a Chromecast idles for hours and a 1st-gen stick has very little memory.
  • The overlay is ~3 s and only on pause or seek. There is no chrome to "show", because nothing can focus it. Everything else is video.
  • Every failure is one sentence, no code, no product. R237's rules apply to a screen with no buttons — which makes them easier: there is exactly one thing to say and nothing to offer.
  • Subtitle size comes from the phone and uses the TV's caption style (white, black outline, R110). Same S/M/L the player sheet sets.
  • It honours Skip Intro and next-episode itself, because it reads the same detail payload and segment markers the TV app does. None of that is possible if the receiver is a dumb screen.
One thing the design cannot answer: whether a CAF v3 receiver still launches on a 1st-generation stick (last firmware November 2022, support ended April 2023). The benchmark is Jellyfin's own receiver on the same stick — if that runs, this will. Identify the generation first: 1st gen is the flat dongle with a rounded end; 2nd/3rd gen are round discs.
