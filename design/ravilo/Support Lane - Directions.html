Round · support-lane · no phase number

Support lane — where a ticket’s state lives while the chat scrolls

One question: a viewer reads a support ticket on a phone. Where do the ticket’s state and its holder sit while the thread scrolls, and how does a change of state appear without the thing stopping being a chat? Three directions below, one carried through every state, then the TV, the admin, the decided rows and the strings. Read left to right, top to bottom.

What is fixed (owner, 2026-10-14) — drawn as given, not reopened

  • Filed on phone, desktop or web — never the TV. The TV shows the thread read-only plus one tap: Same here.
  • Four author kinds: the viewer · AI Agent · Hervør · support · Hallur · admin. Only the last three may post a user-visible message. Other seats receive a forward and never answer into the thread.
  • AI Agent has no name and no face. Hervør is a named AI agent: she carries her name plus the AI badge and her seat badge, so a first name never reads as a human being.
  • A reply sends itself or forwards. No “waiting for approval” the viewer can see. When a reply cannot send itself, AI Agent says “I’ll forward this and they will answer you soon” and the ticket moves to a holder.
  • The viewer sees the real state and the real holder, in user-facing words only — never a phase number, internal name or path.
  • Auto applies to talking, never to acting. A request is logged with an honest “needs the owner’s decision”; acceptance is a human act in the admin. In the drawings AI Agent never resolves anything either — Hervør does.
  • Server-pushed state only. A sent message appears when the server echoes it. The pending instant is drawn as a composer-local state, not as a bubble.
  • Dark-primary, light toggle; purple→blue accent; Space Grotesk · Sora · JetBrains Mono; --ok / --warn / --bad. Admin CSS is production CSS: everything new extends the existing token set, no new file.
How the tension is held. The ticket half lives in exactly two places: the header’s second line (where a chat app shows presence) and the system lines inside the thread (where a chat app says “X joined”). Nothing else is added to the chat. The thing that bends is the ticket: no fields, no status box, no assignee row — state is spoken in presence words and ledger lines, and the only hard “ticket” artefact is the number in the kicker.
1 · The named directionssame thread, same moment: Hervør has just replied
Recommended

A · Presence — the ticket header is a chat header

Pinned. Line one is the ticket (number, inferred class, a short title AI Agent writes from the first message). Line two is presence: “Open · with Hervør · replied in 12 minutes”. The holder’s mark sits where a chat shows the contact. State changes are inline system lines and the presence line follows — you can read the thread without ever looking up, and look up without ever reading the thread.
Direction

B · Ledger — state lives only in the thread

Nothing pinned but the number and the title. Every state change is a system line in the ledger; when the latest one scrolls off the top, it returns as a compact pill under the header. Purest chat. Costs: on first open you must scroll to learn who holds it, and the pill is a third kind of thing between header and message.
The foil

C · Status card — the helpdesk foil

A pinned card of fields — Status, Assigned to, Type, Opened, Last reply, Device — and a plain message list beneath with no system lines. The card mutates silently. Drawn so the owner can see the shape we are refusing: it answers every question at a glance and reads as a helpdesk in the first second.

What A refuses to add

  • No status box, no assignee row, no priority, no SLA clock.
  • No “Ticket #41” as a title — AI Agent writes a short title from the first message (owner, 2026-10-14: the message is never lifted into the title; it stays the first message, and the receipt line says what the case was titled); the number is the kicker.
  • No colour for state beyond the presence dot and the system-line dot: blue while someone holds it, green when resolved, hollow when the holder is a seat that cannot answer, grey while nobody holds it.
2 · The vocabularyauthors, system lines, presence, class chips, composer-local states — one frame
Rules

Reading the marks

Circles are people, squares are software. The viewer and Hallur get round marks with an initial; AI Agent and Hervør get rounded squares — Hervør’s with her initial, AI Agent’s with a diamond and never an initial. Badges: AI outlined on both agents, then the seat badge (support, admin) filled — the AI badge, not the mark, is what stops “Hervør” reading as a human being. System lines sit on a hairline, centred, with a dot that carries the state colour; the holder’s small mark appears inline when the line names a holder. Presence is one line, clamped; it is never the only place a state is told — every presence change has a matching system line that holds the whole sentence. Composer-local strips (“Sending…”, “Not sent”) are never system lines, because nothing the server has not echoed may look like thread state.

No optimistic bubble. The field locks and dims with the text still in it, the send button becomes a static ring, a thin progress line runs along the composer’s top edge, and a composer-local strip says “Sending…”. The bubble appears only when the server echoes it.

The echo brought the bubble, the deterministic receipt — number, inferred class, and the title AI Agent wrote from the message — and the header takes that title. The viewer’s message stays a message. Presence is grey: “Open · waiting for a first reply”. No holder mark yet (dashed).

A bubble-shaped working indicator with three static dots and the words “AI Agent is writing” in the author row. The holder mark becomes the square. (Static in the canvas; in the app the dots may pulse.)
State

AI Agent answered, auto

A plain reply, same bubble as a human’s, the square mark and the outlined AI badge doing the telling. Presence: “Open · with AI Agent”. The class chip has appeared in the kicker.
State

The forward

“I’ll forward this and they will answer you soon” is a normal AI Agent message; the state change is the system line under it, and the header’s holder mark and presence follow: “Open · with Hervør”.
State

A state change as an inline system line

Scrolled mid-thread: “Hervør is handling this” sits between AI Agent’s forward and Hervør’s first message, with her small mark inline. The header above has not moved; it already says the same thing in presence words, with the timing.

Held by a seat that may not answer

Ticket #38. The owner holds it, but the owner cannot post; Hervør owes the next visible message. The presence dot goes hollow, the line says both things, and the holder mark stays Hervør’s — the viewer’s contact is the agent who will write, not the seat that decides.

Resolved

Green dot, “Resolved · by Hervør”, a green-check holder mark, and the composer stays — its placeholder says what typing does: “Write here again to reopen”.

State

Empty — no ticket ever filed

One sentence of invitation and the primary button. Nothing else on the page.
State

Loading

Three skeleton rows in the list’s own shape. The thread loads the same way (header skeleton + two bubbles) and is not drawn separately.
State

Error — send failed, offline

The text stays in the field, editable. The strip above the composer turns to --bad-tinted “Not sent — you’re offline. Your message is kept here.” with “Try again”. Nothing enters the thread, because the server never saw it.
State

Offline

A quiet bar under the header: “Offline — showing what was last loaded”. The thread and header are the last pushed state, unchanged; the composer stays open and fails honestly (previous frame).
State

Longest string — 2 000 characters

One viewer message of 2 000 characters. The bubble clamps at 14 lines with “Show the rest”; the receipt still lands under it and the header carries AI Agent’s short title, so nothing long ever has to fit one line. Nothing truncates on the server side.

5 · The four decisions — decided by the ownerone row each · the pick is drawn in sections 1–4 · the alternative stays here for the record
All four rows were decided on 2026-10-14. Each row names the pick and where it is drawn, keeps the alternative beside it for the record, and notes what the pick changed in the mockups.
Decision 1

Does the ticket header pin above the thread, or scroll away and return as a compact bar?

Decided

Pinned

Direction A — section 1, left phone. The header stays; presence and holder are always one glance up.

Not picked

Scrolls away, returns as a pill

Direction B — section 1, middle phone, and again to the right. The latest state line comes back as a floating pill once it scrolls off.

Decided: pinned. The pinned header is 86px and is the only at-a-glance answer to “who has this”. The pill is a third kind of thing between header and message, and it has to re-explain itself every time it appears.
Not picked · B, scrolled: the pill under the header
Decision 2

Is the class (bug · request · question) picked by the viewer before writing, or inferred and shown afterwards?

Decided

Inferred, shown after the receipt

Every A frame — section 3, row one. The chip appears in the header kicker together with the “Received · #41 · logged as a bug” line.

Not picked

A chooser before writing

To the right: three chips above the empty thread. Honest and small — and the one element that turns the first screen into a form.

Decided: inferred, shown after the receipt. A viewer with a bug does not know it is a bug. Classification is the server’s job and correction is Hervør’s, in the admin rail where class is a dropdown.
Not picked · the class chooser before the first message
Decision 3

On a phone, where does the unread marker live?

Decided

On the Profile entry that already exists

Section 3, row two, “An unread reply”: a dot on the Profile avatar in the app bar and the bottom bar, and a “Support · 1 new reply” row inside the Profile sheet.

Not picked

A new Support item in the bottom bar

To the right: a fifth slot with a red count. On a 393dp phone it displaces Discover or Profile.

Decided: on the Profile entry that already exists — no Support tab on the bar. The bar’s slots are spoken for, and a red support count would outrank Continue Watching on every day there is one reply. The Profile dot is quiet and already where account things live.
Not picked · Support as a bottom-bar item
Decision 4

Does the admin’s Support page sit beside Activity, or take its own top-level entry?

Decided

Under AI, with the count on the AI sidebar entry

Section 4: the AI page gains a tab row (Fetchers · Support); the AI sidebar entry shows the number of open tickets. No new menu item on the left.

Also decided

Activity gets a “Support log” tab — logs only

To the right: a history of what every agent did and every action taken, one line each. Nothing on it is actionable; the tickets live under AI › Support.

Decided: no own entry, no own page. Support sits inside AI because AI Agent and Hervør answer the tickets; the sidebar learns to carry count badges so open tickets are visible from the left menu; Activity keeps only the log.
Decision 4 · Activity › Support log — history only

Two things the brief did not decide — we had to draw them one way

Who taps “Same here” on a shared TV?

Drawn: the signed-in TV profile (Ása) adds a line to Jákup’s ticket about that TV. If tickets are strictly private per viewer, the TV should instead list only the signed-in profile’s own tickets, and “Same here” means “it happened again”.

Does a resolved ticket keep its composer?

Drawn: yes, with the placeholder “Write here again to reopen”. The alternative is a “Write again” button that opens a new ticket referencing the old number.
