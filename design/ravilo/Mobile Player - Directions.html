Round 1 · directions only · 2026-09-16

The Ravilo player on a phone

Against design brief §A (specs/ravilo/design-brief-mobile-player-and-cast-2026-09-16.md) and the investigation behind it. Today PlayerScreen renders the TV chrome verbatim on a phone — 48 dp gutters, D-pad pills with focus rings, forced sensor-landscape, no gestures, no safe-area reads. Three directions below differ on one axis only: where the secondary controls live, because that is what decides whether the thing is usable with one thumb. Everything else — the waiting states (R218), the failed-start copy (R237), the two-level flag-forward picker (R180/R195) — is reused, not redesigned.

Nothing here is built into Ravilo Mobile.html yet. Pick a direction and the build follows.

These are not choices. They come from specs that already shipped, or from the mockup's own floors.

Decided — 16 September 2026

Direction 2 · Thumb rail is the build. Owner's pick, together with every lean on the decisions panel: auto-hide 3 000 ms; the player follows the sensor and offers a rotate button only when the system has portrait locked; both brightness and volume swipes; Subtitle size as a row inside the picker sheet; haptics on skip, lock and seek release; the mobile-data quality row drawn next; and phase 111's Play on Stue TV drawn beside the cast button in round 2.

Playback speed is removed entirely — owner decision, 16 September 2026. Not a rail item, not a sheet, not a More entry, and no seam member: the one control on this list that needed new plumbing in RaviloPlayer plus a Wasm actual plus a later iOS actual is simply not being built. The rail is therefore three items — Subtitles · Next · Lock — which also gives each one more room. Two strings (pl_speed, pl_speed_normal) come off the list, leaving thirteen.

The axis the three directions were drawn on

All three show the same four things — position, transport, audio & subtitles, episodes. They differ in how far your thumb travels to reach the secondary controls, and how much picture the chrome eats. On a 915 × 412 landscape frame held in two hands, the reachable zones are the two bottom corners and the right edge; the centre-bottom is the worst place for a small control and the best place for a big one.

Why 2 won: it is the only one where Audio & subtitles — the most-used control in a trilingual household — stays one tap and lands under the right thumb, while the seek bar keeps the full width and clears the home indicator. Its cost is real: the right-edge rail is vocabulary the TV app does not have, and the Skip Intro pill has to move left of it.

What I did not draw, deliberately

No thumbnail scrub previews (trickplayUrl is always null — there is nothing to show). No PiP, no background audio, no lock-screen card for local playback (owner decision). No AirPlay affordance, ever. No brightness/volume sliders — only the transient pills a swipe produces.

Direction 1

Grounded — the TV's bottom block, made touchable

Everything in the bottom third, exactly as the TV orders it: position, then transport, then the secondary row. Nothing new to learn and nothing new to name; the transport pills simply grow to 46 px and lose their focus rings. The cost is the crowding — four controls and a seek bar in 190 px means the secondary row is a text strip, and in portrait it stacks under a letterboxed picture with the home indicator right beneath it.Cheapest to build. One layout branch on LocalHandset, no new components. Also the least like any phone video app the household already uses.
Direction 2 · CHOSEN

Thumb rail — transport in the middle, secondary on the right edge

The big three (−10 s · ▶ · +30 s) sit centred on the picture, at the size the gesture equivalents want to be anyway. The seek bar keeps the full bottom width. The three secondary controls become a right-edge column: Subtitles, Next, Lock — one tap each, all inside the right thumb's arc. In portrait the same three rotate into a row above the seek bar, same order, same icons.New vocabulary. The rail does not exist on TV, and the Skip Intro pill must move to the rail's left so the two never overlap. Worth it: subtitles stay one tap.
Direction 3

Sheet-first — almost no chrome, one More button

Top bar, seek bar, play, and a single More that opens one sheet holding subtitles, episodes, lock and quality. Double-tap carries the seeking, so even the skip buttons go. The most picture, the calmest frame, and it scales without change to Live TV and to the casting remote.Two taps to subtitles. In a house that switches between Faroese, Danish and English mid-episode that is the wrong thing to make deeper. Kept on file as the direction to fall back to if the rail tests badly.

Auto-hide

The TV's 3 600 ms is right for a remote you put down. On a phone the chrome is summoned by the same tap that could be a mis-tap, so 3 000 ms is my proposal, with two exceptions that hold it open: a sheet is open, or a drag is in progress. Nothing auto-hides while Locked.

Where the twelve states are drawn

All twelve of brief §A1 are drawn for Direction 2 below — landscape first, then the sheets in portrait, then the iOS safe-area section, then the same chrome in Midnight and Noir. Directions 1 and 3 are drawn once each, above, which is enough to choose between them.

Direction 2 · all twelve states of brief §A1

Every state the recommended direction has to hold

Landscape below, portrait after it. The two states that are not really states — chrome visible and chrome hidden — bracket the rest: everything else appears over one of them and leaves again.
State 2

Chrome hidden

The default. Video only, no scrims, nothing over the picture. One tap brings the chrome back; a tap on a control never toggles it away.
State 3a

Double-tap to seek

A ripple on the half you hit, the amount in it, and repeat taps accumulate (10 → 20 → 30) while the ripple stays up. Chrome does not rise; this is not a request to see the controls.
State 3b

Swipe for brightness / volume

Vertical drag on the left half is brightness, on the right half volume, each with a transient pill at the edge it belongs to. Both drawn together here; only ever one at a time.
State 3c

Pinch to fit / fill

One word, centre screen, 900 ms, then gone. The only thing a pinch may do — it never zooms the picture to an arbitrary scale.
State 4

Locked

Everything hidden but a lock glyph above the home indicator. A tap says "Locked · hold to unlock" for a second. Auto-hide is suspended, gestures are dead, the system back is not.
State 5

Scrubbing

Knob grows to 26 px, a time bubble rides above it, the ghost bar shows where you came from. No thumbnail — the server has none, and a blank tile is worse than no tile.

Decided — every row settled

All nine were answered on 16 September 2026: my lean on eight of them, and Speed removed outright on the ninth. Recorded here as the build's instructions rather than as questions.

1 · Chrome direction

Where the secondary controls live — the whole round.

2 · Thumb rail
2 · Auto-hide delay

The TV's 3 600 ms is for a remote you put down; a phone's chrome answers a tap that may have been a mis-tap. Held open while a sheet is open or a drag is running; suspended entirely while locked.

Both. Neither exists in any seam today — there is no brightness API in the codebase — so both are new work, and both are conventions every phone video app has made invisible.

Dropped — not built

On skip, on lock, on a seek release. Cheap, and the thing that makes a transport feel mechanical rather than painted.

Round 2, with the cast button

What the two follow-ups mean

Quality on mobile data. The investigation's sharpest finding is that a phone on cellular gets no bitrate cap at all — linkKind = "unknown" means phase 177's cap never applies, so an LTE phone direct-plays a 93 Mbps remux onto someone's data plan. The viewer-facing half is one Settings row (Auto · Data saver · Always high) and a Wi-Fi only switch — prospective phase 218 + R244.

A "Play on Stue TV" action. Phase 111's remote-play routes already exist; in a house where every screen runs Ravilo this is arguably a better "cast" than Chromecast and costs no Google SDK. A detail-screen affordance, so it lands with brief §D in round 2 — next to the cast button, not instead of it.

What is deliberately not in this round

  • Background audio, PiP, a local mini-player, offline downloads — owner decision or explicitly out of scope.
  • Any AirPlay affordance — dropped outright, 2026-09-16. Not drawn, not designed, never.
  • Thumbnail scrub previews — trickplayUrl is null on every ticket; there is nothing to render.
  • Lock-screen / notification controls for local playback — R193 says the phone never creates an OS-level media session. The casting notification is a different thing and is drawn in the casting canvas.
  • Home, Detail, Browse, Search, Discover, Live TV guide, login — brief §D, the second round, waiting on the picks from this one.
  • Tablets. Every frame here is a handset; LocalHandset is the switch that keeps both the TV and a future tablet layout untouched.
