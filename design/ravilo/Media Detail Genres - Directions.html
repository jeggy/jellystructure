Showing every genre on a Ravilo detail page

Today the hero meta line renders item.genre — one string, one genre, dropped in between the year and the age rating. Most titles have three or four. The question isn't only where the other genres go; it's what genres are for on this screen.

Two honest answers, and they pull in different directions

Genres are description

Part of the same glance as year, HD and the age badge. Quiet, unclickable, there to answer "what kind of film is this?" Cheapest, no D-pad cost.

Genres are navigation

The way you leave this title for a shelf of others like it. Ravilo already has the destination — the browse page with its Genre facet — and R190 set the precedent by making a cast face a link into it.

Genres are identity

One genre is what a film is; the rest are footnotes. TMDB returns them in a meaningful order, so the first can carry real weight and the tail can stay small.

Direction A takes the first answer, B the second, C the third. All three are drawn on the real detail hero at half scale, using Ravilo's own stylesheet.

The constraint that shapes all of this: this is a 10-foot D-pad screen. The hero already holds four focus rows — synopsis, actions, and on a series the season pills and episode rail. Every genre chip that becomes focusable is another stop on the way down the page, and Down from the title should still land on Play. Only B pays that cost, and it pays it deliberately.

Direction A

All of them, in the line they already live in

The genre slot simply stops being one string. Genres render as text separated by hairline dots, in the same weight and colour as the year — no chips, no new row, nothing focusable. Past three, the tail collapses into a +2 pill in the same style the subtitle flag strip already uses. Cheapest by far and impossible to get wrong. The cost is that the meta line is already the busiest thing in the hero: format, year, four genres, an age badge and an IMDb score in one 19px row. It reads as a spec sheet rather than as anything you'd want to look at.

Direction B

Their own row — and a way out of the page

Genres leave the meta line and become a labelled row of their own, built exactly like the AUDIO / SUBTITLES flag strip directly below it: a dim 13px caps label, then the values. The pattern is already in the design language, so this reads as native rather than added. Each chip is focusable and opens the browse page seeded to that genre — the same contract R190 gave a cast face. The first chip is tinted with the accent because TMDB's order is meaningful: this is a Drama that is also three other things. One extra focus row on the way down, placed above the synopsis so Down from the title still reaches Play in the same number of presses as today.

Direction C

One genre is the identity, the rest are detail

The kicker line above the title already exists and currently repeats the tagline. Here the primary genre takes it, in the accent blue small-caps the kicker is already styled in — DRAMA · 2 SEASONS — so the first thing read after the title is what kind of thing this is. The remaining genres sit under the meta line as one quiet, unemphasised line. The most editorial and the least chrome: nothing gains a border, nothing gains focus, and the hero gets calmer rather than busier. It's also the most opinionated — it asserts that genre one matters and genres two-to-four are footnotes, which is true of Offboarding and arguable for a Comedy/Horror.

What I'd ship

Direction B, with one borrowing from C: keep the accent tint on the first chip, so the row still says which genre is the primary one. B is the only direction that turns genres into something you can act on, and Ravilo already owns the destination — the browse page's Genre facet, reachable today only from a row's See-all. A genre chip is the natural front door to it, and it costs no new screen.

Why it looks native rather than bolted on: the labelled-row pattern is lifted wholesale from the audio/subtitle flag strip immediately below it — same 13px dim caps label, same gap, same row rhythm. Nothing new is invented; an existing pattern gets a second use.

Behaviour to pin down

  • Cap at four visible chips, remainder in a focusable +N. Never wrap the row to a second line on TV — the hero has no vertical room and a wrapped row pushes Play below the fold on a 720p panel.
  • Order is TMDB's order, never alphabetical. The first genre is the primary one and gets the accent tint; re-sorting would destroy the only signal we have.
  • The label is singular when there is one genre ("Genre"), plural otherwise — and the whole row is omitted, not left empty, when a title has none.
  • OK on a chip opens browse seeded to that genre, scoped exactly as the person filter is in R190: Back returns to the detail page with focus restored to the same chip. +N opens browse with the title's full genre set applied.
  • Focus order: genres sit between the meta line and the synopsis. Down from the genre row reaches the synopsis, then the actions — one extra press to Play than today, which is why the row goes above the synopsis rather than below the buttons.
  • Rows and tiles are out of scope. A poster tile's subtitle keeps showing one genre — four would not fit and the tile is not where this question gets asked.

Open questions

  • Does jellystructure already store the full genre list per item, or does item.genre flatten it at scan time? The admin's Metadata page manages genres as a set, so the data is likely there — but the Ravilo payload needs checking before anyone builds.
  • Genres are TMDB's English names. Ravilo is translated (en/da/fo) — do the chips need localised genre names, or is this the one place English leaks through? TMDB returns localised genre lists per language, so this may fall out of the metadata-language work for free.
  • Should the accent tint on the primary chip appear at all in Noir, where the accent is amber and the tint reads as a warning colour?
