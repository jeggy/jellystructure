The page that opens when a film ends

A new page, drawn before its UI is built. It reuses Tile, ArrowRow and the title page's backdrop scrim; this round draws the layout only.

Round after-film-page · 2026‑11‑27 · Android TV, phone, desktop, Ravilo Web · product design

What is fixed

Everything in this section came with the brief or out of a shipped file and is not this round's to move.

  • What the page is. When the player would close a finished film, the after-film page opens instead. It shows up to six unwatched titles from that film's own More Like This list. Picking one opens that title's page and never plays it at once (owner, 2026‑11‑27). Nothing plays and nothing loads a stream until the viewer picks. One Back leaves to the finished film's page.
  • The words. The kicker End of film with the film's title, and the heading More Like This. Both strings already ship, in all three languages. This round adds no new string.
  • Every component. The shipped tile (.tile.poster on TV, .poster on the phone, .po on desktop and web), the shipped row, the shipped kicker, and the title page's own scrim — --hero-tint over --hero-floor, both skin tokens.
  • Focus. Exactly one visible focus target: the first tile. Focus is a class on TV (.focused), never a pseudo-class — a D-pad has no hover. The treatment is the shipped 4 px ring plus scale(1.07), unchanged.
  • QoE first (owner, on this ask). No new animation anywhere, and focus motion is the shipped transform and nothing added to it. Nothing on this page triggers a network request: the More Like This list arrives with the film's own page.
  • Jellyfin, Seerr and jellystructure are invisible to a viewer. Nothing in this round names them.

Question 1 — the film's title as text, or its logo art?

Direction B — the logo when the film has one, text when it has not Recommended

This is the brief's lean, and it is already how two shipped surfaces behave: .dhero-logo on the title page draws the logo and falls back to .dhero-title, and the phone player's .mp-ident carries a logo and a name variant of the same slot. A viewer who has just finished a film recognises its logo faster than its name set in the UI's own face, and the fallback costs nothing because it is direction A.

  • Gives: the film is identified as itself rather than as a row of UI type, and the page reads as the end of that film. No new component and no new string.
  • Costs: two code paths where there was one, and a logo's fine detail dies at phone size — see the landscape frames, where the stand-in's sub-line is a smudge at 52 px.

Direction A — the title as text, always

  • Gives: one path, one size, legible at every scale in every language. The Faroese frames show it holds.
  • Costs: the page identifies the film the way a settings row would. On a surface whose whole job is you have just finished this; here is what is next, that is the one place worth spending a little boldness.

What the pictures cannot tell you. The corpus has no logo file for an invented title, so direction B is drawn with a wordmark stand-in — a drawing standing in for artwork. Judge the placement and the size, not the artwork. A real logo is wider, looser, often not a wordmark at all, and sometimes dark ink on transparency (which the Studios tiles already handle with a light plate, .taxo-card:has(.taxo-logo), R257/R308). Whether this page needs that plate is not settled by this round.

Sizes, and why they differ

The logo is drawn at 96 px on TV, not the title page's .dhero-logo 116, and the text at 56 px, not .dhero-title's 72. On the title page the identity is the subject; here the subject is the row, so both step back. The two numbers are not equal to each other because they do not measure the same thing: a logo's height includes its mark and whatever line sits under the word. At 96 the word is about 42 px tall against direction A's ~40 px cap height, so the two are drawn at the same legibility and differ only in character.

Question 2 — does a pointer client need a visible back control?

Use the window header's own back Recommended

The brief's lean was a ghost Back top-left on web and desktop. Both are drawn, and the lean turns out to add a second back control to a page that already has one: header() in design/ravilo/desktop/ravilo-desktop.js already puts a .ghb center bar with a back chevron at the top of .main whenever the navigation stack is not empty. The header variant also names the film in the bar, which the floating button does not.

  • Gives: one back control, in the place every other page in the window keeps it, with the film named beside it. Nothing new is built.
  • Costs: the backdrop loses its top 46 px and the page is no longer full-bleed. The Daylight frames show the hard edge between a light chrome bar and the dark page.

A ghost Back on the page

  • Gives: a full-bleed backdrop, and a control the eye finds without leaving the page.
  • Costs: two back controls on one page in the normal case, and a button floating on artwork with no bar to belong to.

On the phone, and on the TV

The phone keeps a visible back in both orientations: a touch surface has no Back key and no Esc, so the circle is the only route. In landscape it sits top-right — at top-left it landed squarely on the kicker and the logo, which the rendered frame showed and the markup did not. The TV draws none, as the brief leans: the remote has the key, and a second focusable thing in frame would break the constitution's "exactly one visible focus target".

The light theme, and the TV

The TV has no light theme to draw. design/ravilo/platform/ravilo-shell.js holds TV_SKINS = ['aurora','midnight','noir'] and prints "The TV is dark-only: it shows the viewer's dark pick (Aurora here)" for any light pick; i18n/en.json carries theme.dark_only = "This TV is always dark". Noir is rendered in its place, because it inverts the accent to amber and is the skin that actually tests the kicker.

On the phone and on desktop, Daylight leaves this page dark, and that is correct rather than a failed render: a surface that sits on artwork keeps the dark tokens (html[data-appearance="light"] .hcard, .mp in ravilo-phone.css, and .lt .hero .hb on desktop). This page is entirely artwork. What does change in Daylight is the chrome around it — the rail, and the window header in the recommended variant.

Loading, error and offline — why they are not drawn

This page requests nothing. The More Like This list arrives with the film's own page, which the viewer was on before the player opened, and the brief's own rule is that nothing loads a stream until the viewer picks. The one thing that can be absent is poster artwork, and every tile in every frame here is already the no-artwork tile. If the implementation turns out to fetch the list at this moment rather than carry it, that is a loading state nobody has drawn — and it is a question for the spec, not a gap this round can fill by inventing one.

Open — for the briefer

1. What happens when nothing in the list is unwatched? "Up to 6" includes 0, the brief names no such state, and the spec excerpt does not say. b-tv-empty.png is what the page looks like if it opens with nothing to offer: a heading over nothing, and no focus target at all — which the constitution's "must never strand focus" forbids outright. The frame deliberately prints no sentence, because inventing one would be deciding this. A lean, not a decision: the page does not open, and the player closes to the film's page as it does today.

2. Does the app bar belong on this page? Not named in the brief. b-tv-appbar.png draws it so the choice is a picture. Recommended: no. The page came out of fullscreen playback and offers one decision; a nav bar puts four more targets in frame and draws Library as current, which at three metres reads as a second focus.

3. Does a dark logo need the light plate? R257/R308 found 88 of 135 production logos are dark ink on transparency and gave the Studios tiles a light plate for it. This page puts the logo on a film's own backdrop, which can be anything. Out of scope here; worth a line in the spec.
