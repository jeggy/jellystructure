Coming Soon — stacking a series' same-day episodes

Three directions for the one card, on the TV, the phone and the desktop. Drawn in this repository on 2026-10-22; 45 rendered frames in png/; sources beside this file.

slug coming-soon-stacks · Ravilo · TV 1920×1080 · phone 412×915 · desktop 1440×900 and 560×900

What is fixed

  • One picture per series. The calendar holds a series poster and backdrop and no episode stills for unaired episodes, so every layer of a stack is the same art or a tint of it.
  • A stack is one focus stop on the TV, and Back from whatever it opens returns focus to it. Nothing changes the row's height.
  • Server-pushed state only. Every number a stack shows is a number the server can send; nothing on this page is derived or optimistic.
  • One card component across the three surfaces in the shipped app — so a direction that needs a different structure per surface costs three implementations, and this document says when one does.
  • All three skins. Read-and-playback only: nothing here offers to edit, re-scan or repair.
  • The date strip, the day rows, the All / Series / Movies chips and the Missing (N) pill are unchanged except where the stack itself necessarily changes them.

The three directions

A — Stacked edge recommended

Today's card at today's size, with two offset edges of the series' own art behind it, the episode range as the subtitle, and the count in the bottom corner beside the air time. The card's footprint is unchanged — 430 px on the 1920 stage, the same as every other card in the row. The front face gives up the 32 px the deck occupies, so the row's rhythm, its height and the number of cards in it are all untouched.

B — Wide list card

The art and the body keep their shipped geometry and an episode list sits beside them. B cannot honour "nothing may change the card's width": a list of eight episodes needs room. What it keeps is the height and the row's baseline; what it spends is two card slots — 878 px where A spends 430.

C — Count only

The shipped card, unchanged, plus the range in the subtitle it already has and the count badge. No new geometry at all. Drawn because "there is only one picture anyway" is a real argument for doing nothing to the art — and because it is the control that shows what the deck in A actually buys.

Why A. The occasion for this round was a row that stopped reading as "a season arrived" and started reading as noise. A fixes exactly that and costs the row nothing. B answers a different question — which episodes — and answers it best precisely where the problem is smallest (a mixed or overdue stack), while being at its most redundant on the uniform whole-season drop that prompted the round. C is honest and cheap and does not fix the thing that was wrong.

The six decisions

1 — Shape

A, B, or C. Lean: A, for the reason above. B is worth keeping in view for the Missing row specifically, where it is clearly better; that is a legitimate answer of "A in the day rows, B in the Missing row" if the owner wants it.

2 — When a stack forms

Lean: two or more episodes of one series on the same day, whatever their times; in the Missing row, by series regardless of day. The alternative — three or more — is drawn below, and it leaves the two-episode pair as two near-identical cards, which is the same problem at a smaller scale.

3 — What selecting a stack opens

Lean: the detail page each of those cards opens today — Series detail for a series we hold, the not-held detail for one we don't, with the stack's episodes listed in its schedule. Drawn below with the two states that have no picture behind them yet.

4 — What the date strip counts

Lean: cards — what the row shows. The alternative counts episodes, and then Today reads 9 while the row holds two cards.

5 — Which time a stack shows when its episodes differ

Lean: the earliest. A range is legible and honest; a count of times reads badly beside a count of episodes.

6 — What the badge says on a mixed stack

Lean: "{k} of {n} available", as a --warn pill rather than a green one — warn is this system's "mixed" semantic, and the words carry the state as well, because state is never colour alone. A downloading episode keeps its own percent chip.

What this document is not

It is a set of directions and six open questions. It allocates no number, writes no spec, and decides nothing the owner has been asked to pick. The findings — including two places where the brief contradicts itself and three where the shipped token files are short of what this needs — are in REPORT.md beside it.
