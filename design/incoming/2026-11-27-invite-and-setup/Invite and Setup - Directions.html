The invite sheet, the landing page, and setup from an invite

Round 2026-11-27-invite-and-setup. Three surfaces in one round: the admin’s Invite a viewer sheet, the public /join landing page, and the server-setup screen when it arrives pre-filled from an invite. 74 pictures. The round’s question — what comes first on the landing page — is answered in §2 with three drawn orders.

1 · What is fixed before anything is drawn

  • The link carries no credential. It is the server’s address and nothing else, and it never sets that address on its own: the app shows it and the viewer presses Connect.
  • A TV cannot be invited. No camera, no browser. Every surface therefore ends with the same sentence — on a TV, open Ravilo and type <host> — and the typed setup gets better instead.
  • The landing page is one self-contained document. Inline CSS and SVG, no token file, no second stylesheet, no image, no hosted font — the shape SingleSignOnPages already ships. Every colour and size in the page frames was read out of that Kotlin and written into invite-data.js.
  • The admin sheet is wf.css. It reuses the shipped .pair-back / .pair-modal modal at its shipped 440 px, and both themes are drawn.
  • The setup screen already exists, and this round carries its geometry unchanged from round 2026-11-19-setup-hidden-characters so the two rounds’ pictures of one component can be diffed. What is new is four things: the from-invite line, the not-found line, the waiting button, and the example in an empty field.
  • Exactly one visible focus target per frame, and no new animation anywhere. QoE first.

2 · The question: what comes first on /join?

Three orders, drawn at iPhone 16 width with the browser bar, so the comparison is of pages and not of descriptions. Every one of them keeps the host block first and the TV line last; what moves is the three actions between them.

OrderTop to bottomGivesCosts
L1 — Open in Ravilo firstrecommended open · get-app · browser · tv A viewer who has the app is one press from done. A viewer who does not sees it fail softly, and the next thing on the page is the fix. The page’s first button is the one that does nothing on a phone with no app — and it is also the only button whose failure is silent.
L2 — Get the app first get-app · open · browser · tv The page leads with what always works. Four buttons before the one press most invited viewers need, and the page’s single primary then sits in its second group, under a list.
L3 — Browser firstthe spec as written browser · open · get-app · tv The one route that needs nothing installed and no second step. It sends the viewer to the browser client when the app is the better player — and it is the order FR-3 already states, so choosing L1 or L2 needs a dated amendment.
The answer, as a recommendation: L1. Not because of the lean in the brief, but because of something the pictures settled that prose could not: the whole page fits an iPhone 16 viewport with 23 px to spare. Every frame carries its own measured overflow in the caption. Nothing on this page is below the fold on any phone drawn, so "first" is not about what a viewer sees — they see all of it — it is only about which button is the primary and which is an outline. On that question L1 is right: the primary should be the action most invited viewers will take, and that is opening an app they already have.
L3 is what the spec says today. FR-3 lists the parts as Watch in your browser → Open the Ravilo app → Get the app → TV. An engineer builds FR-3, not the brief. If Bárður adopts L1, FR-3 needs a dated amendment in its own file, and it is a one-line one.

3 · The Android button, after the owner’s 2026-11-27 change

Android is one button, labelled like the others, and it carries no “open this link again” line, because it never needs an install: it links to the Ravilo Web address, which opens the installed app through its App Link filter and Ravilo Web on any other phone. macOS, Linux and Samsung TV stay downloads and keep that line.

So the line belongs to the download group and not to the section — that is what the change actually moves, and it is why the group is drawn as one web button, then macOS and Linux two-up, then Samsung TV, with the sentence under those three alone.

There is no iPhone button, and that is deliberate rather than an omission. The brief’s section head reads Android, iPhone/web, desktop, but iOS is in the spec’s Out list and FR-3 names exactly Android, macOS, Linux and Samsung TV. An iPhone’s route is the browser part, which is its own section with its own label.

5 · The admin invite sheet

Two directions in the shipped 440 px modal. Both carry the link with Copy, a real scannable QR, the TV line and the account line; they differ in which of the link and the code is the primary.

DirectionGivesCosts
A1 — link firstrecommended The link is one unbroken line at 440 px, Copy is full height beside it, and the code is 148 px and centred under its own caption. Copy is what an admin uses most — a link pasted into a chat — and it is at the top. About 150 px taller.
A2 — code first Shorter, and the code reads as the point of the sheet. The link wraps to two lines and Copy drops below it, which is three objects where A1 has two. The code is the same 4 px module but visually smaller beside text.
A requirement the drawing found, not the brief: the QR’s version has to be chosen from the link’s length. A QR at version 3-M holds 42 bytes. https:// + host + /join is 13 bytes of fixed text, so a host over 29 characters does not fit and the encoder must move to version 4. This is not theoretical: the first render of this round failed on a 37-character host. FR-2’s shipped QrEncoder.encode must not be fixed at one version.

6 · The setup screen

Four new things in a column this round does not otherwise touch. The from-invite line sits above the field and the hidden-character warning below it — which is the one thing the brief asked to see together, and the one thing only a picture can settle.

9 · What is not drawn, and why

  • Offline / lost connection. Deliberately absent: FR-7 and R280 say the not-found line must not tell a typo from a device that is offline, and the setup screen holds no socket yet. Drawing a separate offline state would draw a distinction the spec forbids.
  • The slow loading case is the same frame as Trying… — the probe has one 8 s timeout and no intermediate state.
  • Danish and Faroese for the landing page, on the brief’s instruction. The drafts are in §8.
  • Tizen and the Chromecast receiver. Out of the spec’s scope, and a TV cannot take an invite at all.
  • An iPhone download button. §3.
