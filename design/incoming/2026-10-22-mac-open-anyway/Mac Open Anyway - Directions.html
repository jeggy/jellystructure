Opening Ravilo on a Mac, in pictures

Two surfaces: the Mac card on the public download page, and the window the disk image opens to.

round mac-open-anyway · drawn 2026-10-22 · lane C, drawn in the repo · no phase number

Ravilo for Mac is signed with our own certificate and is not registered with Apple, so macOS refuses every hand-downloaded copy twice — the disk image when it is opened, and then the installed app. Each refusal is a dialog whose buttons are Move to Trash and Done, and the only way past either is System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Open Anyway. The second approval also asks for the user's password. Today the page names one of those steps, calls it "the first time", and says it in text — so the button it names stays invisible.

What is fixed

  • The page is dark — #16181f, no light theme — and keeps its existing style.
  • The three download cards are one grid row, repeat(auto-fit, minmax(260px, 1fr)). Anything that makes the Mac card much taller than Android and Linux drags that row with it.
  • Download .dmg stays the card's primary action, where it is.
  • The steps are open by default, and every picture's alt text carries the whole instruction.
  • Never "the first time" — it happens on every download.
  • Never xattr, spctl, or turning Gatekeeper off. There is one correct route and it is Open Anyway.
  • Drawn, never screenshots. No real user name, no second application in any list, nothing from anybody's library.
  • A Finder background image cannot change with appearance. One flat picture must read in Light and in Dark.

Direction A · The strip below the download grid recommended

Handle: A · The strip below the download grid

The Mac card keeps its one-line note and its button. The five steps are a full-width strip under the three-card row, two steps per line, each picture at its full 236px design width. The three cards stay the same height as each other and the Download button stays where a returning visitor already expects it.

Direction B · The strip inside the Mac card

Handle: B · The strip inside the Mac card

This is what the spec's own sentence describes today — the card's one-line note becomes the strip. Drawn so the cost is visible rather than argued, and the cost is two things at once.

B fails on its own terms, and the drawing is what shows it. A card is one column of a three-column grid, so a picture inside it has 96px. At 96px the dialog in step 1 is a grey smudge and the Privacy & Security pane is unreadable — which means the pictures, the entire point of this round, stop carrying the instruction and the sentences are doing all the work again. B is drawn, and not recommended.

The toggle

Handles: Toggle 1 · Always open, no control and Toggle 2 · Under a How to open it summary, open by default

Toggle 1 is recommended. Folded, the page says less than it does today: the one honest line it has now is replaced by a control that hides everything. The folded state is the whole argument — a reader who collapses it is exactly the reader about to meet the first refusal.

The disk image window

Handle: Window · Targets above, steps 4 and 5 beneath

Steps 1 and 2 are not drawn here: by the time this window is open they are behind the user. The window carries the drag (step 3) and then the two steps that have not happened yet — each with its own picture, at the same 236px design width the page uses, so this surface keeps the same promise the page does: every step has a picture and one short sentence. Laying them in two columns is what makes that fit; stacked rows would force a half-size picture, which is the unreadable smudge direction B was rejected for.

The two grey bands are the whole trick. Finder draws each icon's text label itself, in a colour this image cannot see and cannot predict — near-black in Light, white in Dark. So the band under each icon is a flat mid-tone (#777e8d, about 4.1:1 against white and 5.2:1 against near-black) and carries nothing of ours. Everywhere else in the picture the text is ours, so its contrast is ours to choose.

The numbers the engineer places the icons from

Finder window content area720 × 520 pt
Background image720 × 520 px at 1×, 1440 × 1040 px at 2× — one image pixel per point
Icon size128 pt
Ravilo icon centre(190, 190) pt from the content area's top-left
Applications centre(530, 190) pt from the content area's top-left
Label bands (do not cover)190 × 34 pt, centred on each icon's x, top edge at y = 252 pt

A 128pt icon centred at y=190 ends at y=254, and Finder prints the label immediately beneath it — which is why the bands start at 252 and not lower. The window never exceeds 800 × 600 pt, so it opens whole on a 1280×800 screen with the title bar above it.

The macOS button names stay English in all three languages, and that is a decision, not an oversight. The pictures show an English macOS, because that is the Mac the behaviour was walked on. A reader running a Danish or Faroese macOS sees Åbn alligevel, not Open Anyway. Translating the button names in our text would make the words disagree with the pictures beside them; leaving them English makes the text disagree with that reader's screen. Neither is free, and it is named in the report as open.

Alt text, in full

Every picture carries the whole instruction, so the page still works with no pictures at all — which is the state drawn in a-desktop-noimg rather than asserted here.

  1. Step 1 of 5: a macOS dialog refusing the downloaded disk image by name. Its two buttons are Move to Trash and Done. Choose Done.
  2. Step 2 of 5: the Privacy & Security pane of System Settings, showing the downloaded .dmg file named on a line saying it was blocked to protect your Mac, with an Open Anyway button beside it. Choose Open Anyway, and the disk image opens.
  3. Step 3 of 5: the opened disk image, showing the Ravilo icon and an arrow pointing to the Applications folder. Drag Ravilo onto Applications.
  4. Step 4 of 5: the same macOS dialog, now refusing Ravilo itself after it has been installed. Its buttons are Move to Trash and Done. Choose Done again.
  5. Step 5 of 5: the Privacy & Security pane again with its Open Anyway button, and a password prompt with an empty password field. Choose Open Anyway and enter your password; Ravilo opens.
