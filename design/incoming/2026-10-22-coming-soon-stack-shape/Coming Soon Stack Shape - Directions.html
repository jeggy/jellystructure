Coming Soon — what a stack looks like

Round 11, second revision. Four directions for the shape of a stacked card on the TV, the phone and the desktop, drawn after the owner saw A, B and C and picked none of them. A appears once, as the comparison. Every number in every picture is derived from one array in cs2-data.js.

What the owner fixed, and nothing here reopens

1 · When a stack forms. Two or more episodes of one series on the same day. In the Missing row, grouped by series whatever the day. There is no longer a threshold option in the data file, because there is no longer a question.

2 · Selecting a stack opens what each card opens today. Round 11's detail frames stand and are not redrawn here.

3 · The date strip counts episodes. The eight-episode drop adds 8 to Thursday, which reads 9 beside a single episode. Every direction below therefore puts the episode count on the card, so the strip and the row agree.

4 · A stack shows its earliest air time, and a mixed stack says {k} of {n} available, with a downloading episode keeping its own percent. Both facts are readable off all four directions.

The four directions

D — Season band recommended

A solid band across the lower third of the art carries the numeral at display size, the plural noun beside it, and the earliest air time at its right end. The band is a flat surface rather than a scrim, so the numeral has guaranteed contrast whatever the artwork behind it happens to be — which matters, because there is one picture per series and it cannot be relied on for anything.

Cost to the row: none. 430 px wide, 242 px of art, unchanged under focus and in every row. What it spends is 86 px of artwork.

E — Episode rail

One segment per episode along the art's top edge, each carrying its own state: held, arriving, or still only scheduled. It answers the mixed stack exactly — which episodes, not just how many — without a list and without a second card slot. The count badge is enlarged to 26 px, because the rail says "several" and the badge is E's whole answer to "how many".

Cost to the row: none. 10 px of art at the top, and the chip row moves down 18 px to clear it.

Above 12 episodes the rail collapses to one proportional bar, k of n filled. See the spec note below.

F — Focus reveal

At rest the card is the shipped card plus a small count chip, so a row with six stacks in it stays as calm as a row with none. Focused, the whole lockup — number, plural, rail — arrives inside the same 430 × 242 footprint. Nothing grows and nothing reflows.

Cost to the row: none, resting or focused. But the cost is not geometric: at rest F is direction C, which the owner has already seen and not picked. Compare tv-f-today with round 11's tv-c-today — they are the same card. The question asks about a glance, and F only answers on focus.

G — Tall season row

The number lives above the art in a header of its own. G is the only direction that covers no artwork at all, and the only one that shows the number and the per-episode rail together at full size.

Cost to the row: no slots — the card keeps its 430 px — but the row grows 79 px, and every card in that row grows with it, because a row of ragged-topped cards is worse than a taller row. On the 1920 stage that is roughly one fewer day row per screenful, and it applies only to rows that contain a stack, so the page's vertical rhythm becomes data-dependent. That is the thing R367 removed, arriving by a different door.

Why D

The question is what reads at a glance from three metres as this series is arriving with N episodes. At that distance a viewer reads size and contrast, not detail — so the answer is a numeral large enough to survive, on a surface flat enough to guarantee it. D is the only direction that does that at zero cost to the row. G does it as well and costs 79 px of row height for an artwork gain the calendar cannot spend, because there are no episode stills behind it. E answers a different and narrower question beautifully, and stops answering it at thirteen episodes. F is C until you focus it.

The honest caveat: D gives up 86 px — more than a third — of the only picture the card has. On a real poster that is the bottom third of the art, and tv-d-today is the frame to judge that on. If the owner's answer is that the artwork may not be covered, then G is the direction, and the 79 px row is its price.

One combination not drawn, because it is not mine to decide: D's band has room along its lower edge for E's rail, which would give D the per-episode mixed answer at no further cost. It is a legitimate fifth direction and it is named here rather than quietly built.

What a spec would have to say

  1. The band's numeral is the episode count of the stack, and the plural noun beside it is a separate string from the count string. Splitting {n} episodes on its number works in these three languages and silently breaks in the fourth. Fallback: a language that cannot take a bare plural renders the whole up.episodes_count in the word's slot at the word's size.
  2. Per-episode marks are drawn up to 12 episodes. Above that the rail is one proportional bar with k of n filled and the count carries the number — measured on tv-e-24, where 24 segments are thinner than the gaps between them and at three metres read as a solid bar anyway. This applies to E and to G.
  3. A downloading segment's trough is the neutral grey, not an amber tint. The amber is the fill only.
  4. A stack is one focus stop and the ring is one ring, round the card face — or, in G, round the header and the art together.
  5. The band is anchored at both ends. Where there is no air time — the Missing row — the status pill takes the right-hand slot rather than leaving the band hollow.
  6. Nothing changes the row's height or width under focus in D, E or F. In G the row is taller than a row with no stack in it, permanently.
