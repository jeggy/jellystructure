Ravilo as the TV's home screen

Where the Apps entry sits in the TV header, what the Apps screen looks like, and how Apps is reached from the two screens Ravilo can show in place of Home. Android TV only, 960 × 540 dp on the 1920 × 1080 stage, D-pad, Aurora skin unless a frame says otherwise. Drawn 2026-11-17. Forty-three PNGs in png/.

What is fixed

None of this was decided by this round. It is the shipped product and the spec, and every direction below is built on top of it unchanged.

Where the entry sits — three directions

RECOMMENDEDA2 — a labelled Apps pill in the right cluster

The brief offered two; this is a third, and it is the recommendation. It takes A's position — the right cluster, immediately before Search — and B's label, in the shape the bar already has: SurprisePill's, which is navCorner + 6 radius, a 1 dp hairline at rest, navPadH + 2 by navPadV, a 7 dp gap and the label at navType SemiBold.

It exists because the slim-header round settled the identical question the other way round for Surprise me: a glyph-only circle "says nothing", and a labelled pill in the right cluster does not read as a fifth tab. Drawing A and B without A2 would have handed the owner a choice the house has already made once, in the opposite direction.

Frames: A2-standard-at-rest, A2-standard-home-apps-focused, A2-slim-home-apps-focused, and the three-way compare-A-vs-B-standard.

AA round button beside Search — the brief's lean

The same circle Search uses, with a four-rounded-square glyph. Cheapest in width (58 dp more bar than today, against A2's 110) and the quietest at rest.

Its cost is that a bare glyph does not say what it opens. The counter-argument, and it is a real one: four squares in a 2 × 2 grid is a near-universal convention for "apps" in a way that a sparkle is not — which is exactly why Surprise me needed its word and this might not. The glyph is deliberately four squares and not nine dots: nine dots in a 22 dp circle puts each dot under 4 dp, which at three metres is a smudge.

NOT RECOMMENDEDB — an Apps pill at the end of the nav pills

Drawn as the brief asked, in a tab's own padding, corner, weight and inverted-fill focus — because anything else there would be a third treatment inside a row of four that already has one.

The at-rest frame is what decides it. In B-standard-at-rest, Apps is indistinguishable from Movies, Series and Music: it reads as a fifth section of Ravilo's library rather than as the way out of Ravilo. In bar-geometry the focused Apps pill and the focused Home tab are visibly the same object.

B does buy one thing the other two cannot: a place in the bar's "where am I" row, so the Apps screen can mark itself in the bar (B-grid6-apps-first-view). A and A2 have nothing in the bar that can say "you are in Apps", and rely on the page's own title. B2 (B2-slim-home-apps-focused) is B with the glyph before the label; it helps a little and does not change the verdict.

The Apps screen — three layouts

Eighteen entries: Android settings, Google TV, and sixteen apps A–Z. The space a layout has is the screen minus the bar, the 24 dp DiscoverScreen gap and the page head: 408 dp under a Slim bar, 396 dp under a Standard one. Everything here is arithmetic from apps-data.js; layout-arithmetic prints it.

CellRowsVisible without scrolling
GRID 5the brief's lean160 × 90 dp 415 of 18

Six across is recommended, and the reason is not density. Three rows of six is every entry on one screen: nothing below the fold, no scroll position to remember, and no viewer who has to discover that Apps scrolls at all. Five across pushes Twitch, VLC and YouTube under the edge from the very first frame (compare-grid5-vs-grid6).

And the caveat, because it is contingent and not a property of the design. Sixteen apps is the brief's stand-in count. A TV with twenty-five apps needs five rows at six across and scrolls anyway; six across then still halves the scrolling, but "everything in one view" stops being true. The empty band under the third row in grid6-apps-first-view is where app nineteen goes.

Direction C, the list, is a genuine contender and not a straw man. Every name is in Sora at 19 sp with nothing ellipsised and nothing depending on a banner being legible — which is the one honest worry about any banner grid, since a real TV banner is whatever the app shipped and many carry no readable word at all. It also shows all eighteen. It costs the artwork, and with it most of the way a viewer recognises an app at three metres without reading. A single-column list was ruled out by arithmetic before it was drawn: at a 10-foot row height it shows 5 of 18 and scrolls three times, giving up the only thing a list is meant to win.

What is in a cell, and what is not

  • No label under a banner. FR-11 says a banner or an icon and a label. The first render put a label under all eighteen and every banner tile then said its own name twice.
  • The fallback's icon and label sit inside the banner's own cell, so every cell stays exactly 16:9 and the rows stay rows (Downloader, VLC).
  • The fallback cell carries the shipped hairline. --card on --bg is a 4 % step and without it the two apps Android has no banner for read as two apps that had failed to load.
  • The two fixed entries are a card, not a coloured plate — --card-2 under the hairline, the surface Ravilo already uses for "a control, not content" — with the glyph and the label together, because a bare gear is not a name.
  • Focus is the tile treatment, ring and scale and lift. The lift is load-bearing rather than decorative: a banner's colour belongs to the app, and in noir the ring is amber, so a focused orange plate lost its ring until the lift went back on.

The exits — FR-12

The footer row that FR-12 needs already exists. TvSignInScreen has an 84 dp row holding Retry (in the error state) and Sign in with a password instead. Apps joins it as a third ghost button with the same glyph as the header entry and the same word. One control, one name, through the whole flow.

The same answer serves the profile picker and server setup, which share the shape, and it is independent of which header direction wins — so the exits are not blocked on that decision.

Two questions for the owner, both drawn both ways.
  1. Does Apps come before or after the password button on the can't-reach screen? Signing in with a password cannot work while the server is unreachable, and Apps is the only control on that screen that reaches Wi-Fi. Drawn as cantreach-apps-focused (after) and cantreach-apps-second (before, straight after Retry).
  2. Does the can't-reach screen keep Retry as its arrival focus? My lean is yes — Retry is the way out of the error and Apps is one Right press away (cantreach-retry-focused). This is a behaviour question, not a drawing one.

The first tile's label

The brief leans TV settings over Android settings, because Ravilo has its own Settings and the viewer needs to know this is not it. In English the decision is not a fit — both sit in either cell. first-tile-label is the 1:1 crop.

In Faroese neither fits a six-across cell. Sjónvarpsstillingar is nineteen characters and one word, so it cannot even wrap; Android-stillingar is eighteen and also ellipsises. 131 dp holds about fourteen characters at 13 sp. So the label choice does not rescue six across, and the two are not independent decisions. At five across both fit whole.

A third way, not drawn and offered as an option: let the two fixed tiles' label wrap to two lines. Android-stillingar would break at its hyphen and fit; Sjónvarpsstillingar still would not.

Skipped, and why: a loading state. FR-11's list is read off the TV's own PackageManager each time Apps opens — it is local, it is not a network call, and nothing in Apps comes from jellystructure. A skeleton would be drawing a wait that does not happen. If the build finds the query slow enough to see on the Streamer, that is a new frame and a one-line amendment, not a redesign.

Decided in the drawing, and why

DecisionWhy
A's position is immediately before SearchThe right cluster is where the bar keeps its actions, and nothing that already ships moves to make room.
The Apps glyph is four rounded squaresNine dots do not survive 22 dp at three metres. Squares are also the one shape Ravilo's own content never uses, so the glyph cannot be read as a grid of posters.
Grid cells are 8 dp radiusThe 12 dp content-tile radius eats a banner's own corner artwork; 6 dp reads as square.
A list row takes the inverted fill aloneIt is a chip, not a tile. The first render drew the fill and a ring, which a D-pad user reads as two targets.
The signed-out Apps screen reuses the sign-in screen's 46 dp brand rowA signed-out TV has no app bar, and a second brand row would be a second design.
Column-major order in the listDown each column is the alphabet, which is the axis a D-pad user holds.

Canvas and render notes

  • frames.html links ../../ravilo/ravilo.css rather than copying it, so every token, skin and family in the pictures is the production one. Every class here is ha- prefixed, so nothing can collide in either direction.
  • No CSP <meta>, and none is needed. DEB-2922 made render-design.sh answer ravilo.css's Google Fonts @import from design/app/fonts/. The brief's note about a file:-only CSP is stale.
  • No content-visibility, no backdrop-filter, no large blur, no infinite animations. Two production fills are drawn flat and said so: accentDim (the resting primary button) is the accent under a darkening plate rather than a filter, and the ghost button's border is rgba(255,255,255,.26).
  • Every SVG id is minted from a counter — forty-three frames each carry their own brand mark.
  • apps-qr.js and verify-qr.py are verbatim copies of the TV sign-in round's, so the sign-in frames carry a real code rather than a drawn one. The round's string decodes back exactly; a physical scan is still owed, as it was there.
