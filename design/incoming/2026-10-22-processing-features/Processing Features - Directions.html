Processing as nine features

The scan pipeline configurator is removed and nine named features take its place, each with its own switch, its own interval and its own state. This round draws what that means on the four surfaces that have to agree about it — Settings, the Scan dialog, Activity and a film or series page — at desktop width and at 412 px.

Round 1 of two. Everything here is drawn in full so there is something to push against: both widths, both themes, every state named in the brief, and the alternative to each open decision. Nothing is picked. Round 2 is the owner working from these.

These are the real components. The frames link the shipped wf.css and app.css, so every token, button, badge, toggle, segment and card in the pictures is the one in production. What is new is in processing-features.css and is named to be promoted as it stands.

What is fixed, and is drawn that way

  • Everything switched on runs by itself. A new or changed title gets every enabled feature at once; everything else is re-checked when its own interval says it is due. There is no screen anywhere that arranges work in an order — that order is in code.
  • The server sends every value. No count on any screen is worked out by the page: no "3 of 9 due", no summed queue total, no countdown. Checked by inspection, frame by frame — and once that rule is taken seriously, the obvious summary strip at the top of Settings cannot be drawn at all, so it is not there.
  • Telling the media server is not a switch. It happens after every write, and Settings says so in a sentence instead of offering a tick.
  • Waiting while a TV plays is one household switch, at the top of Settings, not one switch per feature. Work an operator starts never waits.
  • A part that only queues work is marked as such, everywhere it appears, and the scan says so when it ends.
  • A control that cannot do anything new is shown, disabled, with its reason. Never hidden, never live and useless.

The judgement calls the brief left open

Music or audiobooks not mapped: shown and explained, not hidden. A feature that vanishes makes the admin wonder whether it was ever there; a greyed row with one sentence ("No audiobook library is mapped. Map one under Libraries and this switches on.") answers the question where it is asked. The cost is one inert row.

One name for the measurement on every surface. The brief called it A/V sync in the dialog and the lane, while the product calls it Sound in step with the picture on a title. The round uses the product's name everywhere and shortens it to "sound in step" only where a column forces it. One model, one name.

What this round could not settle

  1. The repair kinds under "Make files play directly" are working copy. Four are drawn — Move the index to the front, Pull subtitles out of the file, Repack the audio, Drop a track no player can read — and they are placeholders. The real kinds are in the code and nobody wrote them into the brief. The layout does not change when the names do.
  2. Per-library feature switches have a natural place, and it is not where it was expected. Not in Settings ▸ Processing, where it would multiply nine rows by four libraries — but on the library card under Libraries, as a short "this library skips:" line, next to the Scan button that already scopes a run to that library. That is round 2's question; nothing here designs it.
  3. A running feature in the Schedule table's "Last ran" column reads oddly. running · Twelve Winters S2, episode 4 of 10 is not a "last ran" value. It is drawn that way because the scheduled-task tables this pattern comes from do the same, and the alternative — a sixth column — costs more than it fixes. Worth one look in round 2.
  4. The dialog's queue-only legend is a paragraph. Six of the parts carry a queues work badge and a sentence explains it once. If the explanation has to move into the row, every row gets taller; it was left as a legend deliberately.
