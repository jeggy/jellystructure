One schedule picker, for every recurring job

Round schedule-picker, revision 1 · 2026-11-12. Admin only, desktop browser. One control, built once, in two homes: the auto-fetcher side panel and a Settings ▸ Processing feature row. Pictures in png/, built by frames.html.

What is fixed before a line is drawn

  • The unit menu's order: Off first where a setting can be off, then hours, days, weeks, months, years, and Custom (cron) last. N runs 1–100.
  • Weeks are seven weekday toggles, Monday first, any number on, at least one.
  • Hours take a minute past the hour; every other unit takes a time, with the zone named beside it.
  • Months are a day (1–31 or Last day) or an ordinal weekday. Years are a month and a day.
  • An N above 1 on days, weeks, months or years brings in starting [date].
  • Under the control always: the server's sentence and the next five runs. Save is held while the server says the schedule is invalid.
  • The admin renders server-pushed state only, so the sentence and every date in these pictures is the server's answer — nothing here is worked out in the browser.
  • Type, tokens, radii and the component vocabulary are the shipped admin system. The control reuses .input, .select, .seg, .menu, .btn, .field and .toggle and invents one thing.

The one thing invented

.sch-days, the weekday toggles: .seg's exact look — same border, radius, fill and .on treatment — but multi-select, which .seg has never been. It needed one change that .seg does not have: a visible divider between two adjacent on-cells. A shipped .seg divider is --line, which disappears into --hi-soft; in a single-select seg that never shows, because only one cell is ever on. Here it is the difference between Monday and Tuesday and one wide cell, and it was found at 4× on the dark render.

Decided, with the reason

RowDecided
The foot never collapses. Both halves are drawn in every state, including Off and both refusals. A control that changes height as its own mode is chosen moves under the hand using it.
A refused schedule shows no runs at all. Not the last valid schedule's times. Stale times under a refusal are a lie about what the job will do, and they are what an implementation gets wrong by doing the easy thing.
The server's refusal is a note, not a badge. Every .badge in wf.css is white-space: nowrap; forced to wrap it gives each line fragment its own border, and the 92-character refusal came out of the first render as two separate red boxes. It is now the shape processing.css already uses for a sentence that is a problem.
The unit word follows N. every 1 week, every 2 weeks — in the closed select and the open menu, so the two can never disagree. The first render read every 1 weeks in three frames.
The next-runs heading does not promise five when there are none. Next five runs over a list of five; Next runs over the line that explains there are none. The slot itself always stays.
Months ask "a day or a weekday" first. The two answers need different controls, and offering both at once is four selects for one choice.
The time field is 138 px. The shipped widths are 112 and 100. Chromium draws type="time" in a 12-hour locale as 08:00 PM plus a picker button — about 134 px. A live defect in both homes; see the report.
The classes go in wf.css. Two page files need the control today and four more settings join it. A component two page files need is the shared system's.
