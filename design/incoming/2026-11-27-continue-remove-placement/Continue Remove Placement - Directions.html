Where “Remove from Continue Watching” sits on the title page

Round continue-remove-placement, 2026-11-27. Android TV, the phone, and desktop / Ravilo Web. Three options, drawn in English, Danish and Faroese, in both states plus the state where the action does not exist, with the TV in Aurora and Noir and the phone and desktop in Daylight.

What is fixed before the round starts

  • One ghost button, nothing else. No new component, colour, icon or animation. Every control drawn here is the shipped ghost: .btn.ghost on TV, the adopted phone row’s ghost on the phone, .bt on desktop.
  • Play keeps first focus, the action is never the first focus target, and nothing before it in the row moves.
  • The restore label is the undo. A viewer who removes by mistake presses the same place again, so whatever shape the action takes has to be the same shape in both states.
  • The label flips at once on press, so the control has to be readable in place — it cannot be somewhere the viewer has to go back to.
  • Three states: in Continue Watching → Remove from Continue Watching; removed → Show in Continue Watching; neither → no action at all.
  • The rows the action joins are already full. On TV: Play · Mark watched · Trailer · My List (no Mark watched on a series). On the phone: Play · Download · My List, with My List reduced to a 44 dp icon and Download on a fixed width per language, as the owner settled it. On desktop and Ravilo Web: Play · My List.

The measurement that decides the question

The brief asks whether the row can take one more action at Faroese width. Measured, the answer is narrower than that and the same in every language. The three options

A · Last in the row — the brief’s lean

The action is the last item of the action row, after My List, on every surface.

  • TV: good. 1373 of 1792 px at the longest language, 419 px to spare. Play keeps first focus and nothing before the action moves. ab-tv-film-remove-actionfocus-fo.png
  • Phone: it takes Play’s words away. At 372 px the row is over by 205, and the control that gives way first is the primary one: Play renders as “▶ …” in English and Faroese and “▶ Fo…” in Danish, while the new action is itself cut to “Tak burtur ú …”. Two labels destroyed to add one. a-phone-film-remove-fo.png
  • Desktop / Web: the new label is cut to nonsense in all three languages — “Remove from C…”, “Fjern fra Fortsæt …”, “Tak burtur ú…”. a-desk-film-remove-fo.png

B · A line of its own — recommended

The action row is left exactly as it is. On the phone and on desktop / Ravilo Web the action is a full-width ghost control on its own line beneath the row. On the TV, where there is room, it stays last in the row — option A’s position, which is the right one wherever it fits.

  • Every label whole in every language and both themes, with 98 px of slack on the phone and 230 on desktop. b-phone-film-remove-fo.png, b-desk-film-remove-fo.png
  • Play, Download and My List are untouched, so the owner’s “Play must not move” holds without a second thought.
  • The remove and the restore are the same control in the same place, which is what makes the restore label an undo. b-phone-film-restore-fo.png
  • The shape is not new: it is the full-width secondary control the phone download round already drew (.pd-dlfull), with the progress fill taken out.
  • Cost: one more tap target on the phone and desktop, and on the phone the synopsis moves down 58 px when the action is present. On TV, nothing changes at all.

C · In the row’s overflow

A ⋯ takes the row’s last slot and opens something that carries the action: a sheet on the phone, a menu on desktop, a panel on TV.

  • It fits everywhere, including the phone, and every label stays whole. c-phone-film-row-fo.png
  • But the undo stops being the same place. Remove is two presses and restore is two presses, through a surface the viewer has to open to find out what state the title is in. The label can no longer flip under the finger that pressed it.
  • It is three designs wearing one name — a bottom sheet, a pointer menu and a TV panel — and the detail page has none of the three today.
  • The ⋯ has to be there in the state where there is no action, or the row’s last slot appears and disappears with the playstate. Drawn both ways; neither is good. c-phone-film-none-fo.png
  • On TV it adds a focus stop that says nothing and an overlay the page does not have, against “focus motion is unchanged”.

Recommendation: B. It is the only option in which every label is whole on every surface in every language, the only one that leaves Play alone, and the only one in which the restore really is the undo — the same control, in the same place, with different words. It keeps the brief’s lean wherever the lean works: on the TV it is option A. Its whole cost is one more tap target on two surfaces, and it costs the TV nothing.

States this round did not draw, and why

  • Loading, error, offline. The action is drawn only from a playstate the page already has; the failure path is a toast owned by another piece of work, not a state of this control.
  • The pressed / in-flight moment. The label flips at once and a second press is ignored until the server answers — there is no spinner and no disabled look to draw, which is itself the decision.
  • Midnight and Graphite. Aurora and Noir bracket the TV (Noir inverts the accent to amber and is drawn); Daylight is the phone and desktop light theme and is drawn.
  • Samsung and Chromecast. Out of this action’s scope.

New strings

Two keys. The Danish and Faroese below follow each file’s own row.continue and action.remove_list wording, which is also what the spec asks for; they are drafts for review.

KeyEnglishDanishFaroese
action.continue_removeRemove from Continue Watching Fjern fra Fortsæt med at seTak úr Halt fram at síggja
action.continue_restoreShow in Continue Watching Vis i Fortsæt med at seVís í Halt fram at síggja

The pictures draw a different Faroese from the table above, on purpose. The brief named its Faroese stand-in exactly — Tak burtur úr Halda fram at hyggja — and a designer does not swap a string the briefer chose. But the shipped i18n/fo.json already has row.continue = Halt fram at síggja and action.remove_list = Tak úr mínum lista, and the shipped file wins wherever a string exists. Read together they give Tak úr Halt fram at síggja — eight characters and 66 px shorter than the stand-in.

Every frame is therefore drawn with the wider of the two, which makes the measurement conservative: what ships will have more room than the pictures show, never less. Both widths are in the study (shipped vocabulary rows). The disagreement is reported, not resolved here.

What the pictures showed that the markup did not

  1. On the phone, option A takes Play’s word, not the new control’s. The arithmetic says “over by 205 px”; the picture says Play reads “▶ …”. Those are not the same finding, and only the second one settles the option.
  2. The phone action row is unreadable in the Daylight theme, today, before this round adds anything. ravilo-phone.css has .btn.play { background: var(--ink); color: #0a0c13 }, and Daylight sets --ink: #15171f — near-black text on a near-black fill, a contrast ratio of 1.09:1. Visible in every Daylight phone frame in this round. Not this round’s to fix, and not caused by it. The desktop does not have it: .lt .hero .hb re-declares white ink because the hero sits on artwork, and the phone detail page is not in that rule’s list.
  3. Option A on desktop first rendered as a wrapped second row — a picture almost identical to option B’s, which would have put two options in front of the owner that look the same and are not.
  4. Option C’s TV panel and desktop menu both first covered the control that opened them. Redrawn anchored to the row so the option is judged in the shape its designer would ship.
  5. The desktop menu inherited the hero’s white-on-artwork ink and came out as white words on a pale panel in Daylight.
  6. One hard-coded Faroese string had leaked into the English measurement. In a round whose subject is how wide a language is, that is the one bug that invalidates the figure it appears in.
