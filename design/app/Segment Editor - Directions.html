Intro & credits — three ways to edit segments

All three are the same fullscreen editor opened from an episode row (or a movie’s detail page) and returning you there. All three edit the five kinds — recap · intro · next-time preview · credits · after-credits scene — carry a per-marker lock that detect_segments will never overwrite, and treat Jellyfin’s own segments as one source among ours, publishing what you confirm back to Jellyfin so every Jellyfin client skips too, not only Ravilo. What differs is the question the screen asks you.

A · Watch and trimThe obvious one, done properly: real playback from Jellyfin, one timeline, drag the handles. Waveform and the detector’s own evidence sit under the bar so you can see why it guessed that. Best when a boundary is genuinely wrong and you need to find the right one.
B · Confirm the cutsNever scrub. Each boundary becomes a card with the last frame before and the first frame after, and you answer looks right or nudge it. Fastest possible pass over a season, and the shape that suits “the detector is usually right, I’m spot-checking it”.
C · Season sheetThe whole season on one shared timeline, so the episode whose intro starts three minutes in is visible without opening anything. Multi-select for bulk apply, lock and publish; clicking a row opens the player beneath. Best for the 200-episode grind.

What I’d ship

C as the front door, A as the room behind it, and B’s two-frame confirm folded into A as the way you accept a boundary. That gives one editor, not three: the season sheet is where you land from a series (it answers “what needs me?” in one look), a click drops into the trim view for the episode that needs real work, and the frame-pair + Looks right control lives at the bottom of the trim view so a confident pass is still two keystrokes per episode. A movie opens straight into the trim view — there is no season to sheet.

  • Locks are per marker, not per title — you almost always trust the credits and doubt the intro.
  • Jellyfin is both a source and a destination. Its own segments (Intro Skipper) come in as a proposal you accept or override; anything you confirm is published to its Media Segments API, so Jellyfin clients skip too. Worth deciding: do we publish everything, or only what a human confirmed?
  • Never say “fingerprint” or “heuristic” to the operator. Every source reads as plain language here — “matched across episodes”, “guessed from black frames”, “from Jellyfin”, “set by you” — while the numeric confidence stays visible for the doubtful cases.
